> [!star] Today's must-reads

> - **[Gemini 3.7 Flash](https://deepmind.google/blog/introducing-gemini-3-7-flash/)** (559pts HN — #1) — _Google's new workhorse quietly leapfrogs the model everyone was waiting for._ Three weeks after 3.6 Flash, Google ships Gemini 3.7 Flash: a coding/agent workhorse that jumps DeepSWE from 49% → 65.3% and FrontierCode 34.4% → 43.6%, at _half_ the price ($0.75/$3.75 per M tokens). The New Stack's headline says it plainly — [the model that scored 65% on DeepSWE isn't the one Google promised](https://thenewstack.io/gemini-3-7-flash-agents/) (3.5 Pro). _The Flash line is where most production agents will actually run — this matters more than the frontier flagships._
>
> - **[DeepSeek Harness developer preview](https://deepseek.com/harness/en/)** (532pts HN) — _DeepSeek open-sources its agent runtime, and "everything is a plugin."_ A Node.js agent harness built around a Cordis kernel that mounts/unmounts every capability — models, tools, skills, sandboxes, loops, scheduling, UI — as plugins. Agent = Model + Harness. _Agent infrastructure is going open the way models did. Worth studying if you build agents rather than just consume them._
>
> - **[GPT-5.6 Sol Ultrafast — 14× faster on Cerebras](https://openai.com/index/previewing-ultrafast)** (388pts HN) — _Inference speed is the new battleground._ OpenAI + Cerebras preview an API tier running GPT-5.6 Sol at up to 750 output tokens/sec — 14× faster. _When the model is a commodity, the differentiator is how fast it returns. A concrete shot in the "inference inflection" the Latent Space crowd keeps flagging._
>
> - **[Spaghettifying DRAM](https://github.com/xoreaxeaxeax/skitter-creek-bath-salts)** (472pts HN) — _A one-line exploit that topples every memory-isolation scheme on the CPU._ Christopher Domas (Black Hat 2026) shows that flipping a single bit in the DRAM controller's address-scrambling register rewires physical memory underneath everything built above it — PSP, SMM, microcode, SEV/SGX/TDX. `xor dword [0xf80c2094],0x00400000` and suddenly `&x != &x`. _A masterpiece of low-level reverse engineering — read the `*p` pipeline diagram alone. The fences guard physical addresses, not DRAM coordinates._
>
> - **[Understanding is the new bottleneck](https://www.geoffreylitt.com/2026/07/02/understanding-is-the-new-bottleneck)** (160pts HN) — _How to stay in the loop as agents write your code._ Geoffrey Litt (Notion) argues understanding agent-written code matters not to verify but to *participate*, and steals from education: code-explainer docs, quizzes as "speed regulators" on the AI loop, and interactive micro-worlds. _The best career-advice-in-disguise this week — a direct counter to the "take yourself out of the loop" crowd._
>
> - **[How AI disrupts engineering progression](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/08/AI-disrupts-engineering-progress/)** (InfoQ, Aug 13) — _The QCon London flip side of yesterday's "middle class of SWE" essay._ Alasdair Allan's talk "Engineering Progression When AI Ate the Middle": AI removes the learning opportunities at each rung while letting people perform above their level — fewer juniors enter, entry-level hiring slows. _If yesterday's essay worried you, here's the talk version with the same uncomfortable conclusion._
>
> - **[What we learned reproducing 2,200 papers from ICML](https://huggingface.co/blog/icml-2026-open-reproductions)** (Hugging Face, Aug 13) — _Reproducibility is in crisis — here's the data._ HF's massive ICML 2026 reproduction effort, with hard numbers on how much of "state of the art" doesn't reproduce. _Calibrates how much to trust any headline benchmark — including the ones above._
>
> - **[Nine PBS sues Iron Mountain over its locked archive](https://current.org/2026/08/nine-pbs-sues-iron-mountain-over-blocked-access-to-archival-data/)** (216pts HN) — _70 years of TV history, locked in a data center._ The public TV station can't access its archival data after its cloud-storage vendor went defunct and the data center now holding it demands terms. _A concrete, human story about who actually owns your data when intermediaries fail._
>
> - **[Where did the old web go? We followed 657,607 links](https://0.mk/blog/link-rot)** (117pts HN) — _A large-scale measurement of link rot._ One researcher followed hundreds of thousands of links to quantify how much of the open web is already gone. _Data for the slow disappearance of the old web — skim it to feel the shape of the problem._
>
> - **[TDD inside the agent loop — theater or actual value?](https://martinfowler.com/articles/exploring-gen-ai/tdd-in-the-agent-loop.html)** (Martin Fowler / Birgitta Böckeler, Aug 11) — _Does telling your agent to write tests first actually help?_ Böckeler ran experiments on whether TDD in the agent loop produces measurably better code. _Empirical and careful — exactly the question to ask before cargo-culting an agent workflow._
>
> - **[Text AI watermarks will always be trivial to remove](https://www.seangoedecke.com/text-ai-watermarks/)** (85pts HN) — _Yesterday's watermark story, from the other side._ Sean Goedecke's counterpoint: text watermarks are trivially strippable, so they can't do what the optimistic framing claims. _Pairs with yesterday's "where a watermark can hide in plain text."_
