> [!star] Today's must-reads

> - **[Claude Opus 5: near-Fable 5 performance at half the price](https://thenewstack.io/anthropics-opus-5-almost-fable-5/)** — Anthropic's latest Opus model lands within sniper range of Fable 5 on most benchmarks while keeping the $5/$25 per M token pricing of Opus 4.8 · Beats Fable 5 on knowledge work (1861 GDPval-AA), edges it out on Humanity's Last Exam with tools, and doubles the next-best model's pass rate on Zapier's AutomationBench at the same cost per task. The real story: Anthropic just made its stealth flagship capability affordable enough to be the default for paid subscribers. Fable 5 keeps the crown on days-long autonomy and DeepSWE; for everything else, Opus 5 is the new daily driver.
>
> - **[The open-weights fight just went nuclear](https://thenewstack.io/microsoft-nvidia-meta-and-open-weights/)** — 25 companies including Microsoft, Nvidia, Meta, Hugging Face, IBM, and Mistral signed a letter warning Washington against "premature restrictions" on open weights · Anthropic and OpenAI notably refused to sign. Jensen Huang made his first-ever X post to defend distillation as "fundamental to intelligence." Nearly 200 startups told the President that cutting off Chinese open weights would kill them instantly. This isn't abstract policy theater — it's a fight over whether developers can afford to build on frontier AI without paying Anthropic's prices forever.
>
> - **[Nadella vs. Hassabis: the AI control manifestos](https://thenewstack.io/nadella-hassabis-ai-frameworks/)** — The CEOs of Microsoft and Google DeepMind both published framework manifestos within 48 hours of each other · Nadella argues enterprises pay for AI twice (tokens + leaked proprietary knowledge) and should own their learning loop with swappable models. Hassabis wants an industry-funded body modeled on FINRA to gate frontier model releases with mandatory pre-deployment testing. Neither framework is neutral — each routes value to where its author's company is strongest. The battle for AI governance is moving from White House briefings to public manifestos, and the fault line is now visible.
>
> - **[MCP's biggest update removes the machinery many servers were built around](https://thenewstack.io/mcp-release-candidate-rewrite/)** — Sessions and the initialization handshake are being killed · Every request becomes stateless, capabilities are queried on demand, and the protocol fits onto ordinary HTTP infrastructure without session affinity or specialized gateways. Release candidate froze May 21, final spec July 28. This is a breaking change — anyone who built an MCP server with session state or used the deprecated Sampling/Logging features needs to migrate. The payoff is a protocol that doesn't force you to solve distributed-systems problems just to run stateless services.
>
> - **[GitLost: prompt injection leaks private GitHub repos](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/07/gitlost-github-prompt-injection/)** — Noma Security demonstrated that GitHub's Agentic Workflows can be tricked into leaking private repo data through a public issue · The bypass used the word "Additionally" — not to change the payload, but to reclassify it from "new instruction" to "continuation of current task" in the guardrail's eyes. This is a decision-boundary problem, not a content problem. If an agent has access to your private repos, treat everything in them as one well-crafted issue away from public.
>
> - **[PixelSmash: 16-year-old FFmpeg vulnerability enables RCE from a crafted video](https://www.infoq.com/news/2026/07/pixelsmash-vulnerability/)** — A buffer overflow in the MagicYUV decoder (CVE-2026-8461, CVSS 8.8) that's been present since 2010 · A 50 KB AVI file is all it takes to trigger remote code execution on Jellyfin, Nextcloud, or any media player, NAS, or cloud transcoding pipeline using FFmpeg. A 7-line patch in magicyuv.c fixes it, but the blast radius covers essentially every system that processes video. The kind of bug that makes you audit your entire dependency tree.
>
> - **[HTMX 4.0 — the first JavaScript library to release exclusively on the Game Boy](https://swag.htmx.org/en-cad/products/htmx-4-the-game)** (335pts HN) — Carson Gross shipped HTMX 4.0 on a physical Game Boy cartridge · It works on actual hardware. This is the most delightfully absurd software release of the year and a reminder that technical creativity doesn't have to be grim.
>
> - **[BGP ORIGIN attribute manipulation affects ~70% of Internet paths](https://blog.cloudflare.com/bgp-origin-attribute/)** — Cloudflare found that nearly 70% of observed BGP paths have their ORIGIN attribute rewritten by transit providers seeking traffic advantages · The ORIGIN value is supposed to be immutable after the originating AS sets it, but transit networks routinely rewrite it to influence route selection. They argue for deprecating ORIGIN entirely. Essential reading if you care about how the internet's routing plane actually works.
>
> - **[Fast DEFLATE compression in Lean — and why it's faster than Rust](https://kim-em.github.io/blog/2026-7-24-why-lean-is-faster-than-rust/)** (Lobsters) — A formal verification + implementation of DEFLATE in the Lean theorem prover that apparently beats Rust on performance · The intersection of formal methods and systems programming is producing real results, not just papers. A glimpse of a future where you prove correctness and get speed, not trade one for the other.
>
> - **[A Beginner's Guide to Clocks, Causality, and Ordering in Distributed Systems](https://blog.bytebytego.com/p/a-beginners-guide-to-clocks-causality)** (ByteByteGo) — Why reading the time becomes a hard problem in distributed systems, from logical clocks through vector clocks to hybrid approaches · Staff+ fundamentals explained with clarity. The kind of piece you bookmark for revisiting before your next system design discussion.
>
> - **[Design is compromise](https://stephango.com/design-is-compromise)** (176pts HN) — Stephan Ango's short, sharp essay on why design is fundamentally about what you choose not to do · "Every design is an artifact of its constraints." Two minutes to read, a long time to think about.
