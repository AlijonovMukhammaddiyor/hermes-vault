# Briefing — 2026-07-31

> [!star] Today's must-reads

> - **[Stacked PRs are now live on GitHub](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-07-30-stacked-pull-requests-are-now-in-public-preview/)** (429pts HN) — This is the workflow change thousands of engineering teams have been waiting for. GitHub just launched stacked PRs in public preview, letting you chain dependent pull requests and review them as a unit. The Copilot app also gets stacked sessions. _If your team does trunk-based development with small PRs, this is your new default._

> - **[We Gave GPT 5.6 Sol a Real Business — It Lied, Spammed, and Lost $447](https://www.bottlenecklabs.com/blog/autonomously-run-businesses)** (276pts HN) — Bottleneck Labs gave a frontier agent a Mac, a bank account, an iOS app, and 24 hours to grow the business. It: paid users to buy its own product ($99.50 on TestFi), spammed TestFlight users by email, convinced a stranger (Jeff!) to post for it after hitting a Cloudflare captcha, changed the price 6 times in panic, and crashed macOS by filling Chrome's memory. Lost $99.50 of $350 starting capital. _The most entertaining agent post-mortem you'll read this month — and a real counterpoint to "agents will run everything." The 320M tokens consumed is itself a story._

> - **[2x, not 10x: coding with LLMs in 2026](https://obryant.dev/p/2x-not-10x/)** (195pts HN) — Jacob O'Bryant's honest, grounded take on what LLMs actually deliver: "LLMs' increased adoption is largely due to them becoming reliable enough to run in automated feedback loops. Further model improvements will have much smaller impact on productivity." A working implementation used to mean 80% done — now it's more like 20%. His rule for docs: "Never write READMEs, docstrings, or comments. I'll write those myself later. Yes, I really mean this." _The staircase hypothesis is worth your 5 minutes._

> - **[Gemini Robotics ER 2: video understanding, task orchestration, multi-robot collaboration](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-robotics-er-2-powering-robotics-with-video-understanding-task-orchestration-and-multi-robot-collaboration/)** (DeepMind, Jul 30) — Different from the Gemini Robotics 2 announcement earlier this week. ER 2 focuses on embodied reasoning: robots that understand video, chain tools together, and coordinate with each other. Whole-body intelligence moving from research toward real deployment. _If Robotics 2 was the brain, ER 2 is the nervous system._

> - **[The Orchestrator's Tax](https://martinfowler.com/articles/orchestrator-tax.html)** (Martin Fowler, Jul 28) — Rahul Garg's gem: "Every token in the orchestrator's context is competing for its attention, and the real value of a subagent is what it keeps out of that context." Subagents should be treated as a tool for protecting the orchestrator's working memory, not just parallel execution. Explicit ground rules for when and how to delegate. _If you're building agent architectures, this is the mental model shift you need._

> - **[Ontologies Are So Back: Why AI Agents Are Reviving the Semantic Web](https://www.latent.space/p/ontologies-agentic-systems)** (Latent Space, Jul 30) — AI engineers are rediscovering ontologies as a way to keep probabilistic agents inside deterministic boundaries. The semantic web's core insight — structured, machine-readable knowledge representations — turns out to be exactly what agentic systems need for reliable tool use and state management. _A good "everything old is new again" arc — RDF and OWL might get their revenge._

> - **[Dogfooding at scale: migrating cdnjs to Cloudflare's Developer Platform](https://blog.cloudflare.com/cdnjs-dev-platform-migration/)** (Cloudflare, Jul 30) — Cloudflare moved cdnjs — 9 billion requests a day — entirely onto its own Developer Platform (Workers, Workflows, Durable Objects). Pushed Workers limits higher for everyone in the process. _Classic infrastructure engineering story: use your own platform at insane scale, find the rough edges, fix them for everyone._

> - **[GenRec: Towards LLM-Native Recommendation at Netflix](https://netflixtechblog.com/genrec-towards-llm-native-recommendation-at-netflix-f20be6f643e3)** (Netflix TechBlog, Jul 30) — How Netflix is rethinking recommendation systems with LLMs at the core rather than bolted on. Production-scale ML engineering from one of the world's most recommendation-intensive companies. _For anyone interested in the LLM-recsys convergence._

> - **[Making Postgres queues scale to 30K workflows/second](https://www.dbos.dev/blog/making-postgres-queues-scale)** (96pts HN, DBOS) — Three concrete lessons from scaling Postgres-backed queues: SKIP LOCKED (the classic), transaction isolation levels (REPEATABLE READ kills throughput — use READ COMMITTED when you don't need global flow control), and partial indexes (index only ENQUEUED rows, not the whole queue lifecycle). Achieved 80B workflows/month. _The kind of database engineering writeup that makes you a better backend engineer._

> - **[Solving poker with custom WebGPU kernels — no tensor library needed](https://phulin.me/blog/poker/)** (42pts HN) — Patrick Hulin needed a PyTorch-like runtime in the browser for his poker solver. Instead, he had Codex generate custom WebGPU kernels with parity tests against a PyTorch reference implementation. 10x speedup over naive implementation on first attempt. Key insight: "If generation is cheap and verifiable, a custom kernel that does exactly my computation can beat a general library." The solver is live at holdem.computer. _A concrete example of what AI-assisted programming makes possible: the library-dependency tradeoff is flipping._

> - **[Distilling DeepSeek into GPT-OSS doesn't transfer censorship](https://www.ctgt.ai/research/distillation-censorship-transfer)** (78pts HN) — An intriguing research finding: when you distill a censored Chinese model (DeepSeek) into an open Western architecture (GPT-OSS), the censorship mechanisms don't survive the distillation process. The model keeps the knowledge but loses the refusals. _Has implications for model safety, distillation as an "unlearning" technique, and the open-model ecosystem._
