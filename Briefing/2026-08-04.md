# Briefing — 2026-08-04

> [!star] Today's must-reads

> - **[Cloudflare: Your agent needs a computer, not a container](https://blog.cloudflare.com/cloudflare-computer/)** — Launches `@cloudflare/computer`, an agent runtime that dynamically orchestrates between fast isolates and full Linux containers. Paired with Workers inbound TCP/gRPC support and cross-language RPC (Python↔JavaScript). _Cloudflare's Agents Week continues to deliver. They're building the primitives for an agent-native web — execution, networking, storage, security — and this is the most coherent vision for agent infrastructure from any major cloud provider right now._

> - **[LLMs reward expertise](https://www.seangoedecke.com/llms-reward-expertise/)** (317pts HN, Sean Goedecke) — The better you are at a domain, the more value you extract from LLMs. LLMs don't level the playing field; they amplify existing skill differentials. The expert-with-AI vs novice-with-AI gap is wider than expert-vs-novice without AI. _Counterintuitive and important. If you're early-career, this is your roadmap: depth first, AI second. The AI amplifies what you already know._

> - **[OpenAI: How we built a realtime system for responsive voice AI in six months](https://openai.com/index/continuous-voice-interaction-with-gpt-live)** — Deep engineering on GPT-Live: turnless speech model, low-latency architecture, streaming audio processing, interruption handling. Ships the practical trade-offs the team made along the way. _If you're building real-time AI applications, this is the blueprint from the team that shipped one of the hardest problems in AI UX. File under "read before you build."_

> - **[200 Milliseconds](https://200ms.thenodebook.com)** (168pts HN) — The time it takes for a page to feel instant to a human. A beautiful interactive essay on human perception, web performance, and why the numbers actually matter. Covers the neuroscience of time perception alongside practical latency budgets. _The kind of article that changes how you think about latency forever. Bookmark it._

> - **[ByteByteGo: LLM Security Basics — The Full Threat Model](https://blog.bytebytego.com/p/llm-security-basics-the-full-threat)** (Aug 3) — Comprehensive map of the LLM attack surface: prompt injection, data poisoning, model theft, supply chain, output handling, adversarial inputs, and more. Diagrams the full pipeline with clear categorization. _If you're deploying LLMs in production, this is your threat-modeling checklist. Print it, stick it on the wall, walk through it with your team._

> - **[Massively Parallel Postgres Backups](https://planetscale.com/blog/massively-parallel-postgres-backups)** (85pts HN, PlanetScale) — How PlanetScale backs up multi-terabyte Postgres databases fast. Parallel workers, snapshot isolation, clever use of their Vitess heritage to parallelize pg_dump across hundreds of workers. _Real production engineering at scale. The kind of problem you don't think about until it's 3 AM and your backup is still running._

> - **[SQLite Critical CVEs or LLM Slop?](https://research.jfrog.com/post/sqlite-critical-cves-or-llm-slops/)** (Lobsters, JFrog) — JFrog investigated "critical" CVEs reported against SQLite. Found most were hallucinated by LLM-powered vulnerability scanners generating plausible-sounding but entirely fabricated security reports. _A fascinating case study in the risks of AI-generated security reports and why human verification still matters. SQLite's response is characteristically terse and correct._

> - **[Don't be a meat proxy](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Aug/3/dont-be-a-meat-proxy/)** (Simon Willison, Lobsters) — "If your job is translating natural language from a product manager into prompts for an AI, your job is a thin layer of indirection that will disappear." Provocative, short, and right. If you're just passing messages between humans and AI, you're the middleware — and middleware gets replaced. _The therapist take: if this makes you anxious, that's the point. Build skills that aren't prompt translation._

> - **[Inference Engineering Masterclass — Philip Kiely & Ali Taha, Baseten](https://www.latent.space/p/inference-eng)** (Latent Space, Aug 3) — Deep technical interview with Baseten (just raised $13B Series F) on autoregressive and diffusion inference engineering. Covers batching strategies, kernel fusion, KV cache management, quantized serving, and the economics of inference at scale. _For ML engineers who want to understand what happens after the model is trained. The inference layer is the new database._

> - **[The Orchestrator's Tax](https://martinfowler.com/articles/orchestrator-tax.html)** (Martin Fowler, Rahul Garg, Jul 28) — Every token in the orchestrator's context window is competing for its attention. Subagents aren't about time saved or parallel execution; they're about protecting the orchestrator's working memory by offloading reasoning it doesn't need to hold. A concrete framework for deciding when and how to delegate. _If you're building multi-agent systems, read this before you spawn another subagent. The real value is what stays out of context, not what goes in._
