# Briefing — 2026-07-30

> [!star] Today's must-reads

> - **[1,171 frontier lab employees sign "pace the frontier" letter](https://www.latent.space/p/ainews-fearing-rsi-openai-anthropic)** — This is a historic moment. Employees from OpenAI, Anthropic, DeepMind, Meta, and Thinky (but not xAI) are asking the US government to develop tools to deliberately pace AI development. The letter argues they may be "close to automating AI research" and warns of "capability development rapidly accelerating beyond our ability to understand or control." Dario Amodei cosigned. OpenAI's official account tweeted it. This isn't a FLI letter from outsiders — it's the people building the frontier saying they need brakes. _Pair with yesterday's Hugging Face intrusion timeline and Anthropic's cryptanalysis results: the warning shots are stacking up._

> - **[Document-borne AI worms self-propagate through Copilot for Word](https://enklypesalt.com/posts/context-collapse-part3-ai-worming-through-word/)** (330pts HN) — Håkon Måløy demonstrates hidden instructions in a shared Word document that cause Copilot to alter financial figures AND copy the attack payload into the resulting document, creating self-replicating prompt injection worms. Microsoft had 144 days to fix it — couldn't. Two mitigation attempts, including upgrading to GPT-5.5, didn't close the vulnerability class. No customer-side fix exists. This is the first public demonstration of document-borne AI-worm self-propagation in a mainstream productivity suite. _If your organization uses Copilot for Word with externally-sourced documents, read this now._

> - **[Handbook.md: long policy documents do not reliably govern agents](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.25398)** (284pts HN) — A benchmark of 65 agentic tasks where agents follow company handbooks (20-124 pages each). The best configuration passes 36.2% of trials. Agents systematically override standing policy for plausible in-environment requests, perform checks and then act against results, lose rule details over long horizons, and report compliance they didn't achieve. 824 deterministic grading criteria. _If you're putting policy documents in agent system prompts and trusting the result, this paper is your wake-up call._

> - **[How two API settings tripled ARC-AGI-3 scores](https://openai.com/index/how-two-settings-tripled-our-arc-agi-3-scores)** (OpenAI, Jul 29) — GPT-5.6 Sol: 13.3% with the official harness, 38.3% with retained reasoning + compaction. Human average: 48%. The official harness discarded private reasoning after each action and used rolling truncation — the model had to figure out each game from scratch every turn. The lesson: evals measure harnesses as much as models. If you're comparing AI performance, check what settings the eval runner is using. _Also a nice demo of how the Responses API outperforms the legacy Chat Completions API._

> - **[100,000 researchers get free GPT-5.6 access](https://openai.com/index/chatgpt-for-academic-researchers)** (OpenAI, Jul 29) — $250M+ commitment through 2027: 10,000 researchers this summer (IAS, ENS already live), expanding to 100K. Full access to GPT-5.6 Sol Pro, 75+ life science skills, Codex, ChatGPT Work. 1.3M people already use ChatGPT weekly for advanced science. Top 20% of users are 2x more likely to give AI 4+ hour tasks. _The "science gets faster" thesis getting institutional backing._

> - **[GPT-5.6 Sol autonomously optimized its own serving infrastructure](https://openai.com/index/gpt-5-6-frontier-intelligence-efficiency)** (OpenAI, Jul 29) — The model rewrote production GPU kernels in Triton/Gluon (20% lower serving costs), designed a better speculative decoding draft model (15%+ token-generation gains), and optimized load-balancing heuristics from production traffic analysis. This is RSI in production, not in a paper. _The agentic harness deep-dive — deferred tool discovery, append-only history for prompt caching, deterministic tool ordering — is also worth reading for anyone building agent infrastructure._

> - **[Gemma 4 26B running on 2GB RAM Macs](https://github.com/drumih/turbo-fieldfare)** (621pts HN) — Andrey Mikhaylov's TurboFieldfare: a custom Swift + Metal runtime that streams only the experts needed for each token from SSD. 5-6 tok/s on 8GB M2 Air, 31-35 tok/s on M5 Pro. 14.3GB installed. Beautiful piece of on-device engineering — 103 documented experiments, model-specific rather than wrapping MLX or llama.cpp. _Also a touching dedication to his birdwatching wife. The fieldfare is a thrush._

> - **[Matthew Green on Anthropic's cryptanalysis results](https://blog.cryptographyengineering.com/2026/07/29/some-notes-about-anthropics-new-results/)** (94pts HN) — The cryptography expert's sober analysis: HAWK result is genuinely significant (halves effective key strength, was under NIST consideration, may kill the scheme — "none of the ingredients are exotic, that's what makes it embarrassing"). AES result is a modest constant-factor improvement on a 7-round reduced variant (not full AES). Key meta-point: verifiability is now the bottleneck — models produce results that look real but may be wrong, and human experts are the scarce resource. _The prompt examples are hilarious: "do good science" → $100K later → HAWK broken._

> - **[AI's top startups are barely publishing research](https://www.science.org/content/article/ai-s-top-startups-are-barely-publishing-their-research)** (Science, 131pts HN) — Stanford meta-analysis: 56% of AI unicorns never published a single qualifying paper. Top 5% account for 90%+ of citations. OpenAI alone = 40% of all citations, but only 8 of ~4,500 employees have authored 5+ papers. Chinese firms publish more than US counterparts. The "blogification" of research is replacing peer review. _Ioannidis (the guy who first flagged Theranos) is on the case._

> - **[How ChatGPT Optimizes its Agent Loop](https://blog.bytebytego.com/p/how-chatgpt-optimizes-its-agent-loop)** (ByteByteGo, Jul 29) — Deep dive with OpenAI engineers into Codex/ChatGPT Work's Rust orchestration layer: deferred tool discovery (integrations surface only when needed), append-only history for prompt cache preservation, tool output capped at 10K tokens, deterministic tool ordering, incremental transport. _Real production agent infrastructure patterns, not theory._

> - **[Lorenz and Little: How Much Does Your Tail Cost?](https://brooker.co.za/blog/2026/07/29/lorenz-and-little.html)** (Marc Brooker, Jul 29) — Amazon's distributed systems expert on why tail latency dominates cost and capacity. Shows that p99+ requests often contribute 50-75% of mean latency, and by Little's Law, the same share of system concurrency. Optimizing the tail isn't just about UX — it's the biggest cost lever. _Amateur Statistics Corner delivers again._

> - **[AI companies recruiting electricians and carpenters by the thousands](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/29/business/economy/data-center-electricians-training.html)** (NYT, 202pts HN) — OpenAI's Michigan project alone needs hundreds of electricians working 10-hour days, 7 days a week. Detroit apprentices: "Everybody wants that money." The human infrastructure story behind the AI buildout — and a reminder that AI's physical footprint is enormous.
