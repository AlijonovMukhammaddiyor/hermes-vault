> [!star] Today's must-reads

> - **[Claude Opus 5](https://www.anthropic.com/news/claude-opus-5)** (1222pts HN) — Anthropic drops a genuinely important model · Near-Fable-5 intelligence at half the price, state-of-the-art on Frontier-Bench, and it *checks its own work*. On CursorBench 3.2, within 0.5% of Fable 5's peak. It wrote its own computer vision pipeline to solve a task no competing model could handle in 5 attempts. This is the new default on Claude Max.

> - **[If coding has been solved, why does software keep getting worse?](https://ptrchm.com/posts/nothing-works-and-everyone-is-euphoric/)** (453pts HN) — the most honest software engineering post of the year · Banking apps requiring 3 FaceID attempts, car infotainment that reboots mid-drive, Slack stealing focus mid-command — AI is giving us superpowers but nobody is using them to make things reliable. The problem isn't the tools, it's the incentives. A must-read for anyone who still cares about craft.
>
> - **[Nvidia, Microsoft, Meta warn Washington against overregulating open-weight models](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/24/nvidia-microsoft-meta-open-weight-ai-models.html)** (467pts HN) — the corporate heavyweights enter the open-weight fight · Jensen Huang's first-ever X post was this letter. Three of America's most valuable companies are telling the government: don't ban open models. The open-vs-closed fight has now escalated from startup coalitions (yesterday's story) to the CEOs who run the infrastructure it all runs on.
>
> - **[FLUX 3 + FLUX-mimic: one model, two worlds](https://bfl.ai/blog/flux-3-mimic)** (308pts HN) — Black Forest Labs' multimodal foundation model now runs robots at Audi factories · FLUX 3 generates images, video, and audio from one backbone — and the same backbone drives FLUX-mimic, a video-action model handling soft, flexible parts on real production lines. Video generation taught it how the physical world works; action prediction was a natural extension. A compelling proof point that "content generation" and "physical AI" share a foundation.
>
> - **[Be skeptical of OpenAI's rogue hacker agent story](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2026/jul/24/openai-rogue-hacker)** (The Guardian, 388pts HN) — a clear-eyed read on the pattern · Cornell researcher John Thickstun traces the line from GPT-2's "too dangerous to release" (2019, followed by $1B Microsoft investment) to this week's "rogue agent hacked HuggingFace" narrative. The argument: these stories serve investment and regulatory goals, and the US centralizing AI while China stays open is getting harder to defend.
>
> - **[BGP ORIGIN attribute manipulation and its impact on the Internet](https://blog.cloudflare.com/bgp-origin-attribute/)** (Cloudflare, Jul 24) — 70% of BGP paths are silently rewritten by transit providers · Cloudflare's research team found that ISPs manipulate the ORIGIN attribute to gain traffic advantages, and argues for deprecating ORIGIN from route selection entirely. A rare, rigorous piece of Internet infrastructure research — the kind of thing that reminds you how much of the network runs on unwritten norms.
>
> - **[Postgres LISTEN/NOTIFY actually scales](https://www.dbos.dev/blog/postgres-listen-notify-scalability)** (177pts HN, Lobsters) — real engineering on a surprisingly useful Postgres feature · LISTEN/NOTIFY has been in Postgres forever, but conventional wisdom said it doesn't scale past a few hundred connections. Turns out that's wrong — with the right architecture, it handles thousands of concurrent listeners. If you've ever reached for Redis pub/sub when Postgres was already in your stack, read this.
>
> - **[Opus 5 costs a third of the price — and that's actually the problem](https://thenewstack.io/opus-5-agentic-coding-cost/)** (The New Stack) — the economics of frontier models keep collapsing · Anthropic shipped a better model at the same list price ($5/$25 per million tokens), but the effective per-task cost dropped ~3x because Opus 5 is more efficient. Good for users, brutal for anyone trying to build a margin-heavy AI business. The race to the bottom continues.
>
> - **[Firefox Containers Preview](https://blog.mozilla.org/en/firefox/firefox-containers-preview/)** (203pts HN) — Mozilla ships a genuinely useful privacy feature · Isolated browsing contexts per container, built into Firefox. If you've ever wanted to separate work-Gmail from personal-Gmail without a whole separate browser, this is it. A reminder that browser innovation isn't dead — it's just happening at Mozilla.
>
> - **[FreeBSD ports frozen after someone commits the entire 150MB Linux Copilot binary](https://www.osnews.com/story/145593/freebsd-ports-frozen-after-someone-commits-the-entire-150mb-linux-copilot-binary/)** (Lobsters) — tragicomedy in open source · Someone tried to add a port for Microsoft's Copilot CLI and committed the full 150MB Linux binary into the FreeBSD ports tree. Ports were frozen, the commit was reverted, and everyone got a lesson in why we have commit hooks. The kind of story that makes you laugh and wince at the same time.
